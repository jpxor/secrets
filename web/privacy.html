Privacy policy

Effective October 20, 2026 · Covers the official Secrets Android app and this website.

The project

Secrets is an open-source project maintained at jpxor/secrets. The maintainer runs no application server or vault storage service and receives no copy of your vault, passphrase or backup credentials.

For privacy questions, use GitHub Issues without sharing private information. Report vulnerabilities through the security reporting policy.

Data on your phone

Service names, usernames and secrets are encrypted in app-private storage. Your master passphrase is not saved. Decrypted records and keys are held in memory while the vault is unlocked; complete memory erasure cannot be guaranteed. Copying an entry places it on the Android clipboard, where the operating system and apps with clipboard access may read it.

Optional fingerprint unlock uses Android's biometric prompt and Keystore. Secrets does not receive or store fingerprints. Backup credentials are protected separately by the device Keystore so encrypted backups can run while the vault is locked.

Google Drive

Connecting Google Drive uses Google Play services for authorization and requests only https://www.googleapis.com/auth/drive.appdata. This lets Secrets create, list, download and delete backups in its private app folder. It does not grant access to your other Drive files.

Secrets uploads encrypted vault snapshots and metadata used to identify and order them. It does not upload your passphrase, decrypted records or biometric keys. Google processes account and connection information, including IP addresses, request times and file sizes, under its privacy policy. The maintainer receives no Google account tokens or backups.

Google API data is used only for backup and restore. Secrets does not sell it, use it for advertising or transfer it to the maintainer. Secrets' use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including its Limited Use requirements.

SFTP and exported files

SFTP backups send encrypted snapshots and file metadata to your chosen server. Secrets verifies its SSH host key. The server receives authentication details and may log your username, IP address, file sizes and connection times. Its operator controls those logs and their retention.

Manual exports save encrypted files through Android to a location you choose. That location or storage provider may upload or retain copies under its own policies. Keep your passphrase separate from your backup files.

Retention and deletion

Local records remain until you delete them or remove app data. Uninstalling or clearing app data can remove your only vault, so back up first. Deleting an entry does not remove it from older backups or exports. Automatic backups aim to retain five encrypted snapshots; providers and servers may keep additional history.

Disconnecting a provider does not delete its backups. Revoke Google access in your Google Account's third-party connections; remove Secrets' hidden app data through Google Drive's app-management controls where available. Revoking access alone does not delete stored data. Delete SFTP backups through your server administrator and exports from their saved locations. Deleting these files can permanently remove your recovery copies.

Diagnostics and support

Secrets has no advertising or analytics SDK and no maintainer-operated crash reporting. Device logs may contain fixed error categories, but exclude vault values, passphrases and provider response text. Android and your backup providers have their own diagnostics policies.

GitHub issues are public and subject to GitHub's policies. Use made-up entries when reporting problems. Never upload a real vault, passphrase, secret, provider token, private key or unredacted diagnostic log.

This website

This site uses GitHub Pages, with no project analytics, forms, external fonts or project cookies. GitHub may process IP addresses and service logs under the GitHub Privacy Statement. External links lead to sites with their own policies.

Policy changes

Updates are recorded in the public repository with a revised effective date. This policy covers the official project; modified builds may behave differently.
